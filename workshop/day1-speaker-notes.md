# Before you go live (15 minutes before)

**Set up your screen**

- Open ChatGPT (or Claude) in its **own separate browser window**. Drag the tab out of your main browser so it stands alone. *Why: when you share only this window, participants can't see your other tabs, emails or these notes.*
- Have your demo **"My Bakery Business" Project already created** with your instructions filled in. *Why: if setup goes wrong live, you can still show the finished version.*
- Open these notes on a second screen, your phone, or printed out.
- Copy the three prompts (Project template, Readiness Check, Weekly Tasks) into a note so you can paste them into the Zoom chat quickly.
- Turn off notifications (WhatsApp desktop, email, calendar pop-ups). *Why: pop-ups can appear on top of a shared window.*

**How to share only the ChatGPT window in Zoom**

1. Click **Share Screen** (green button at the bottom of Zoom).
2. In the box that opens, **do not choose "Screen" or "Desktop"**. Instead, click the thumbnail of the **ChatGPT browser window**.
3. Click **Share**.
4. Participants now see only that window. If you click onto another window (like these notes), they still see ChatGPT. It may pause or go grey for them, which is fine.
5. To stop, click **Stop Share** (red button at the top of the screen).

*Mac users:* the first time, your Mac may ask you to allow Zoom to "record your screen" in System Settings > Privacy & Security > Screen Recording. Do this **before** the session, because it needs Zoom to restart.

*Tip:* make your browser text bigger before sharing (press **Ctrl +** or **Cmd +** two or three times) so people on phones can read it.

**Send in WhatsApp before you start:** the Day 1 handout image, plus the message "Please log in to ChatGPT or Claude before we begin."

# 0–5 min · Welcome & the 3-day journey

**Why this section matters:** People decide in the first few minutes whether a session is worth their attention. Showing them the finish line (what they'll walk away with) gives them a reason to stay engaged for all three days.

**Say:**

> "Welcome, everyone! I'm so glad you're here. Before we start, type in the chat: your name, where you're baking from, and the cake you're most proud of."

*Why: getting them to type early gets them participating from minute one. People who speak or type early stay engaged.*

> "Here's how the next three sessions work. We're not going to throw a hundred AI tools at you. We're going to take **one real problem in your bakery** and solve it, step by step.
>
> - **Today, Day 1, we Organise and Diagnose.** We'll set AI up properly for your business and find exactly where to start.
> - **Day 2, we Build.** You'll create a tool you can actually use in your business.
> - **Day 3, we Use it and Grow.** We test it, improve it, and make a 30-day plan.
>
> By the end of Day 3, you'll know exactly what to charge for your cakes, and you'll have something you built yourself to prove it."

*Why: you mention pricing here because all of them named it. It tells them "I listened" before you even say so.*

> "Today has three simple steps. They're on the handout I sent in WhatsApp. Keep it open."

# 5–20 min · Step 1: Set up your Bakery Project

**Why we start with organising:** Most people use AI like a stranger. They open a new chat, ask a question, get a generic answer, and give up. That's because AI doesn't know their business. A Project fixes this: they tell AI about their bakery **once**, and every answer after that is about *their* bakery. Organising first means every other step today (and on Days 2 and 3) gives better, more personal results. It's like setting up your kitchen before you bake. You don't start mixing before you've got your tins out.

**Say:**

> "Let me ask you something. Have you ever asked ChatGPT a question and the answer felt... generic? Like it was written for anybody? Type YES in the chat if that's happened to you."

*Pause and read the replies.*

> "That happens because every time you open a new chat, AI has never met you. It doesn't know you sell celebration cakes in Lagos or cupcakes in London. So it guesses.
>
> Today we fix that. We're going to create a **Project**. Think of it as a **folder where AI always remembers your bakery**. You tell it about your business once, and every chat inside that folder already knows you.
>
> It's like hiring a new assistant. On day one you sit them down and explain your business. You don't explain it again every morning."

**Do (share your ChatGPT window now):**

1. Show where Projects are (left sidebar → **Projects** → **New project**).
2. Name it **"My Bakery Business"**.
3. Open the Project's **Instructions** (settings) and paste the template.
4. Fill in your own details live so they see an example.

*Note: the buttons can move when ChatGPT or Claude update. Practise once before the session so you know where they are today.*

**Paste the Project template into the Zoom chat, and say:**

> "I've put the template in the chat. Copy it, fill in the brackets with your own details, and paste it into your Project instructions. Take 5 minutes. Type DONE in the chat when you've finished."

*Why the "DONE" check: you can't see their screens, so this tells you who's stuck without asking everyone to unmute.*

**If someone is stuck:** "No problem, stay with me. Just paste it into a normal chat for now and we'll sort the Project together after the session." *Why: don't let one person's tech problem hold up the whole group.*

**Reinforce before moving on:**

> "So, quick check: why did we set up a Project first? ... Because **AI gives better answers when it knows your business.** Everything we do from now on happens inside this Project."

# 20–32 min · Step 2: AI Readiness Check

**Why this section matters:** Before you fix something, you need to know where you stand. The readiness check gives each baker a **score and one clear starting point** without you having to diagnose everyone one by one. It's also their first experience of AI working *for* them inside their Project, which builds confidence.

**Say:**

> "Now that AI knows your bakery, let's find out where you are. This is like a health check for your business.
>
> In your Project, start a **new chat**. Copy the Readiness Check from the handout or the chat. Next to each statement, type your score from **1 to 5**. 1 means 'not at all', 5 means 'yes, fully'. Be honest; nobody sees this but you. Then send it."

**Do:** Paste the Readiness Check prompt into the Zoom chat. Then do it yourself on screen with your own scores so they see what AI sends back.

> "Look at what AI did. It added up my score, told me my level, and told me the **one thing to fix first**. I didn't have to figure that out myself."

**Ask:**

> "Type your score out of 25 in the chat, and the ONE area AI told you to fix first."

*Why: when they see each other's answers, they notice a pattern, and that sets up your next point.*

**Reinforce:**

> "Notice something? Many of you got the same answer: **knowing what your cakes really cost.** Hold that thought."

*If their answers vary, say: "Different scores, but look how many of us have a low score on question 1, about cake costs."*

# 32–42 min · Step 3: Sort Your Weekly Tasks

**Why this section matters:** The banner promised they'd "decide which repetitive tasks to eliminate, automate, delegate or keep." This step delivers that. It also teaches an important idea: **AI is not for everything.** Some tasks should be stopped, some handed to someone else, and some only *they* should do (like decorating, which is their craft). This stops them from feeling they have to "AI everything", which is overwhelming.

**Say:**

> "Now let's look at your week. Think of the **5 most important tasks** you do every week to run your bakery: the ones that take the most time or bring in money. Things like pricing and quoting, replying to enquiries, buying ingredients, posting on social media, recording sales.
>
> Fill them into the prompt and send it in your Project. AI will sort them into four boxes:
>
> - **Eliminate**: stop doing it.
> - **Automate**: AI can help.
> - **Delegate**: someone else can do it.
> - **Keep**: only you should do it."

**Do:** Paste the prompt into the chat and do your own example on screen.

**Ask:**

> "Type in the chat: which task did AI say to **automate** first?"

**Now read back what they told you before the workshop (no names):**

> "Before today, you sent me your biggest challenges. Here's what I heard:
>
> - 'I need to get my pricing right and not feel bad telling customers my price.'
> - 'I use competitors' prices, but their costs are different from mine.'
> - 'No accurate pricing, no bookkeeping, I want to start properly.'
>
> Do you see it? **Every single one of you mentioned pricing.** That's why pricing is where we start."

*Why read it back: it proves you listened, and it makes the group feel that their problem is shared, not a personal failure.*

**Explain why pricing comes first:**

> "Pricing is the foundation. If your price is wrong, everything else is wrong: you can post every day and get lots of orders and still lose money. And when you **know** your price is fair, because you've calculated it, you stop feeling bad saying it to customers. Confidence comes from numbers, not from guessing."

# 42–55 min · Live Demo: Pricing a Cake with AI

**Why this section matters:** This is the "wow" moment. The banner promised they'd *see* how AI can help, and this is where they see it on a real bakery problem. It also previews Day 2 without giving it away: today they see AI do the maths in a chat; on Day 2 they'll build their own tool.

**Say:**

> "Let me show you what AI can do with your number one problem. I'm going to price one cake, live."

**Do:** In your Project, paste a prompt like this, using **your own real numbers** (prepare them before the session):

> Help me price a cake. It's an 8-inch vanilla cake with buttercream.
> Ingredients (what I paid → how much I used):
> - Flour: 2kg bag for [price] → I used 500g
> - Sugar: 1kg for [price] → I used 400g
> - Butter: 500g for [price] → I used 450g
> - Eggs: crate of 30 for [price] → I used 6
> - [add the rest]
> Packaging (box and board): [price]
> Decorations: [price]
> My time: 4 hours at [your hourly rate]
> Overheads (gas, electricity, data, transport): [amount] per cake
> Add 30% profit.
> Show me a simple table of all costs, my total cost, and a recommended selling price.

**While AI answers, say:**

> "Watch what it's doing. It's working out how much of each bag I actually used, not the whole bag. Most of us forget that, or we forget our time and our gas completely. That's where the money disappears."

**Important teaching moment: check the numbers:**

> "Now, one rule I want you to remember: **AI is your assistant, not your accountant.** Always check its maths. Let me check one line with my calculator..."

*Do one quick calculation on your phone. Why: it teaches them good habits and protects them from AI mistakes when quoting real customers.*

**Then the confidence piece. Paste:**

> A customer just asked me "Why is your cake so expensive?" Write a short, warm and confident WhatsApp reply explaining my price without apologising.

**Say:**

> "This is for everyone who feels bad telling customers their price. You don't have to find the words alone anymore. And now you have the numbers to back it up."

**Ask:**

> "On a scale of 1 to 10, how useful would it be to have this for every cake you make? Type it in the chat."

*Why: it gets them to say the value out loud and builds excitement for Day 2.*

# 55–60 min · Recap, Homework & Close

**Why this section matters:** People remember the beginning and the end. A clear recap locks in the learning, and specific homework makes sure they come to Day 2 ready to build instead of spending session time gathering numbers.

**Recap. Say:**

> "Let's recap what you did today:
>
> 1. **You organised.** You set up a Project, so AI now knows your bakery.
> 2. **You diagnosed.** You got your readiness score and found your starting point.
> 3. **You prioritised.** You sorted your weekly tasks and saw that pricing comes first.
> 4. **You saw AI work** on a real bakery problem.
>
> In the chat, type **one thing you're taking away from today**."

*Why: putting it in their own words helps them remember it far better than hearing you say it.*

**Homework. Say:**

> "Before Day 2, I want you to fill in your **Pricing Recipe Card** for one signature cake. It's on the handout:
>
> - Every ingredient: the pack size, what you paid, and how much you used
> - Packaging and decoration costs
> - How many hours it takes you
> - Your monthly overheads, and what you'd like to earn per hour
>
> Use **your real prices** from your receipts or supplier, not what other bakers charge. Their costs are not your costs."

*Why: on Day 2 they build with these numbers. If they don't bring them, they'll spend the session looking for receipts.*

**Tease Day 2 (don't mention Lovable):**

> "Bring your card with you on Day 2, because we're going to **put it to work**. You're going to build something you can use for every single order. I'll send you everything you need a few hours before."

**Close:**

> "Thank you so much for showing up today. Remember: small steps, big changes. Keep your Project; we'll use it again on Day 2. See you then!"

**After the session (same day):**

- Post a short recap and the homework reminder in the WhatsApp group.
- Note anyone who got stuck setting up their Project, and offer them a quick fix in WhatsApp.
- Send Day 2 setup instructions (including Lovable) a few hours before Day 2.

# Quick Rescue Lines

| If this happens... | Say this |
|---|---|
| Someone can't find Projects | "Just use a normal chat for today and paste your bakery details at the top. We'll set up the Project together afterwards." |
| AI gives a strange answer | "Great, this happens! Just reply and tell it what's wrong, like you'd correct an assistant. You don't need to start again." |
| Someone's on a phone and struggling | "That's fine. Follow on my screen today, and try it on your laptop later using the handout." |
| Running out of time | Skip the "1 to 10" question in the demo, and do the recap in 2 minutes. Never skip the homework. |
| Someone asks about a different problem (marketing, bookkeeping) | "Great question. I'm writing it down, and we'll cover it on Day 3 when we use the same method on your next problem." |
