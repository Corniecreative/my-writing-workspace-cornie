The Price Shock Calculator

Turn a recipe notebook into a true cost per loaf, a safe selling price, and a plan for the next time flour goes up.

Why it works in Nigeria: a 50kg bag of flour has moved between roughly ₦55,000 and ₦75,000 depending on brand and location, and yeast and butter have more than doubled since 2023. Many bakers change prices by feel. This exercise replaces guessing with a number.

When to use it

  • Any time an ingredient price changes
  • Before quoting a big order (wedding, office, school)
  • When deciding between a price rise and a smaller loaf

Steps

  1. Photograph or type the recipe and batch size.
  2. Add what they paid for each ingredient.
  3. Paste the prompt and fill in the brackets.
Prompt to copy 
I am a baker in [city], Nigeria. My recipe for [product] makes [number] pieces:
[ingredient – quantity, one per line]

What I paid:
[e.g. 50kg flour ₦70,000; 1kg sugar ₦___; 500g yeast ₦___; butter/margarine ₦___]

Other costs per batch: gas/diesel/power ₦___, packaging ₦___ per piece, labour ₦___.

1. Work out the cost of ONE piece in a simple table.
2. Give me the selling price at 30%, 40% and 50% profit margin.
3. Show what happens to my cost per piece if flour goes up by ₦10,000 a bag.
4. Compare 3 options: raise the price, make the piece smaller, or adjust the recipe. List the pros and cons of each.
Show your working so I can check it with a calculator.
Follow-up prompt 
Now write a short, respectful WhatsApp broadcast telling my regular customers about the new price. Write one version in simple English and one in Nigerian Pidgin. Thank them, explain the flour increase in one line, and don't sound like I'm begging.

Illustrative figures to demonstrate the method (300g flour per loaf). At a 30% margin the price moves from about ₦1,470 to ₦1,560 when flour goes from ₦70k to ₦80k a bag. Every baker's real numbers will differ. The value is seeing it for their own loaf.

They leave withA cost-per-piece table for their bestseller, three price options, and a ready-to-send price-change message.

Facilitator note: tell the room that AI makes arithmetic mistakes. The line "Show your working" is there so they can check each figure with a calculator, and they should.

The WhatsApp shop that answers itself

Skip the ₦900,000 chatbot. AI writes everything the free WhatsApp Business app already knows how to send automatically.

Most Nigerian bakery orders already arrive by WhatsApp DM. The free WhatsApp Business app has a greeting message, an away message, quick replies (type /price and the whole price list appears) and a catalogue. Most bakers never fill them in because writing them feels like work, so AI does the writing.

When to use it

  • Once to set up, then again whenever prices or menu change
  • Before festive rushes, when DMs pile up

Steps

  1. Run the prompt.
  2. WhatsApp Business → Settings → Business tools.
  3. Paste into Greeting, Away and Quick replies. Add 3 products to the Catalogue.
Prompt to copy 
I run [bakery name] in [area, city]. I sell [products with prices]. I deliver to [areas] for ₦___. Opening hours: [hours]. Custom cakes need [x] days' notice and a [50%] deposit. Payment: [transfer/POS/cash].

Write these for my WhatsApp Business app, in a warm, friendly Nigerian tone:
1. A short greeting message for new customers
2. An away message for after hours that still collects their order details
3. Six quick replies with these shortcuts: /menu /price /order /deposit /delivery /thanks
4. A 2-line catalogue description for each product that makes it sound delicious without over-promising
They leave withA WhatsApp Business account that greets, answers prices and collects orders while they're at the oven. It's set up before they leave the room.

Photo to post, spoken in their own words

Snap a product, send it to AI, and talk about it in Pidgin or English. They get captions and a photo lesson back.

This is what makes the session different: AI works as a photo coach, and nobody fakes a cake. Bakers who don't enjoy typing can just speak. ChatGPT's voice mode understands Nigerian Pidgin, so a baker can describe a cake the way they'd describe it to a customer and get polished captions back.

When to use it

  • Every time a new batch or custom cake comes out
  • WhatsApp Status, Instagram, TikTok, Facebook

Steps

  1. Photograph a product by a window, in daylight.
  2. Upload it to ChatGPT or Meta AI with prompt A.
  3. Tap the mic and describe the cake. Then use prompt B.
Prompt A · photo coach 
This is a photo of my [product], taken with my phone. Be honest: give me 3 simple ways to make my next photo look better using only my phone. Cover light, angle, background and props I already have in a kitchen. Don't change the cake itself.
Prompt B · captions 
Using the photo and what I just said about it, write 3 captions:
1. Fun and playful
2. In Nigerian Pidgin
3. For office, wedding or event bulk orders
End each with "Order on WhatsApp: [number]". Add 5 hashtags for bakers in [city].
They leave withThree posts ready to publish, plus three photo tips that make every future post look better.

Honesty rule for the room: AI can tidy a background, but never post an AI-generated cake you didn't bake. The customer will notice the difference on pickup day, and you lose their trust.

The "Last price?" sparring partner

AI plays the customer who haggles, wants to pay after delivery, or complains about a late cake. The baker practises, and AI scores the reply.

Nobody else teaches this, and it goes straight to profit. Giving in on price and baking without a deposit are where margins disappear. Practising against AI costs nothing, and bakers enjoy it. Put it on the projector with a volunteer and let the room vote on the replies.

When to use it

  • Before a busy season
  • Training a new sales staff member or apprentice
  • After a customer conversation that went badly

Other scenarios to try

  • Wrong name written on the cake
  • Delivery arrived an hour late
  • "My friend sells it cheaper"
Prompt to copy 
Role-play with me. You are a Nigerian customer who wants a 2-step birthday cake for this Saturday. You keep asking for "last price", you want a big discount, and you want to pay after delivery. Be realistic and a bit stubborn. Send one message at a time and wait for my reply.

I am the baker. After 6 messages, stop the role-play and score me out of 10 on:
- holding my price
- getting a deposit
- staying friendly
Then show me better wording for my weakest reply.
They leave withTested replies for their three hardest customer moments, and a deposit policy they can say out loud with confidence.

The 90-day selling calendar

Every sales peak for the next three months, with the product to push and the day to start promoting it.

Most bakers start advertising Christmas cakes in December, after the early buyers have already ordered elsewhere. AI lists the occasions, suggests a bundle for each, and counts back to the start date.

When to use it

  • At the start of every quarter
  • Plan flour and packaging buying around the peaks

Occasions it should catch

  • Independence Day, Christmas, "Detty December", New Year
  • Valentine's, Mothering Sunday, Easter, Sallah
  • School resumption, weddings, end-of-year office parties
Prompt to copy 
Today is [date]. I sell [products] in [city], Nigeria. My customers are mostly [families / offices / students / event planners].

Make me a 90-day selling calendar as a table with these columns:
Occasion | Date | Product or bundle to push | Day to start promoting | One post idea

Include Nigerian public holidays, religious festivals, school calendar dates, wedding season and end-of-year office parties. After the table, tell me which 2 weeks will need the most flour and packaging so I can buy early.
They leave withA three-month plan they can screenshot and pin, with the date to start promoting each occasion.
Take-home extras

Five more wins for the handout

These don't fit in the 60 minutes, but they're easy to follow alone at home. Put them on the back of the handout.

The order form that stops mistakes

AI writes a checklist of questions to ask on every custom order (name spelling, date, pickup or delivery, allergies, deposit). It also writes a confirmation message the customer replies "CONFIRM" to. Fewer wrong-name cakes and fewer no-shows.

Prompt 
Create a WhatsApp order checklist for my custom cake business. It should cover every detail I must confirm before baking, then a summary message the customer must reply "CONFIRM" to, including my [50%] deposit and cancellation rule.

Turn leftover bread into income

Unsold bread is lost money. AI suggests products made from day-old bread (bread pudding, garlic toast, croutons, bread chips), with a simple price for each.

Prompt 
I usually have about [number] unsold loaves at the end of the day. Suggest 5 products I can make and sell from day-old bread using ingredients easy to find in Nigeria. For each: rough cost, suggested price, and how long it keeps.

The two-week sales notebook

For 14 days they write down how many they baked, sold and had left over each day. Then they paste the list into AI and ask which days to bake less. This cuts waste without any software.

Prompt 
Here are my last 14 days: [day – baked – sold – left over]. Which days am I over-baking or running out? How many should I bake each day of the week next week? Explain it simply.

The local flavour lab

AI suggests new products built on local flavours such as zobo, tiger nut (aya), plantain, coconut and ginger. Treat each idea as a starting point and test a small batch first, because AI recipe ratios are often wrong.

Prompt 
Suggest 5 new bakery products using Nigerian flavours like zobo, tiger nut, plantain, coconut or ginger, which I can make with my current oven and equipment. For each: who would buy it, a starting recipe for a small test batch, and a selling price idea.

One-page training card for new staff

They describe their process by voice note, and AI turns it into a step-by-step card with a hygiene checklist to stick on the wall. Useful for every new apprentice.

Prompt 
I'll describe how we make [product] in my bakery. Turn it into a one-page step-by-step training card for a new apprentice, with a daily hygiene and safety checklist at the bottom. Use short, simple sentences.

Golden rules

Five rules to say out loud before they leave

Check the mathsAI makes arithmetic mistakes. Always ask it to show its working, then check with a calculator.
Never fake the cakeImprove the photo, not the product. Only post what you actually baked.
Protect customer detailsDon't paste customers' phone numbers, addresses or bank details into AI.
Test recipes smallTreat an AI recipe as an idea. Bake a small test batch before you sell it.
You stay the bakerAI drafts and you decide. Read everything before you send it to a customer.
