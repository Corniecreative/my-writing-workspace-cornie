Oven to Phone: the UK bakery standard, made doable with AI

In the UK, a small bakery is expected to know its cost per item, label every allergen, keep a daily food-safety diary, answer its reviews, and plan to waste almost nothing. Big chains pay consultants and software for this. This session shows Nigerian bakers how to reach the same standard with a phone and free AI, in an afternoon.

Standards build trustA labelled, costed, hygienic, well-reviewed bakery can charge more and win hotel, supermarket and corporate orders.
AI removes the paperwork barrierThe UK systems are mostly writing and arithmetic. That is exactly what AI does fastest.
Everyone leaves with the documentEach win produces a finished card, label, checklist or message they use the next morning.

The costed recipe card

The UK standard

Every product has a written specification: ingredients by weight, yield, finished weight, cost per item and target profit. Prices change when costs change, not when the owner panics.

The AI shortcut

Photograph the recipe notebook and the receipts. AI builds the card and shows what happens when flour goes up again.

Why it matters in Nigeria: a 50kg bag of flour has moved between roughly ₦55,000 and ₦75,000, and yeast and butter have more than doubled since 2023. Many bakers still price by feel. A written card is also what a hotel or supermarket buyer expects to see behind a quote.

Prompt to copy 
I am a baker in [city], Nigeria. Make me a professional recipe specification card, the kind UK bakeries keep for every product.

Product: [name]. The recipe makes [number] pieces:
[ingredient – quantity in grams, one per line]

What I paid:
[e.g. 50kg flour ₦70,000; 1kg sugar ₦___; 500g yeast ₦___; butter/margarine ₦___]
Other costs per batch: gas/diesel/power ₦___, packaging ₦___ per piece, labour ₦___.

On the card include:
1. Ingredients by weight, yield, and finished weight per piece
2. Cost of ONE piece in a table
3. Selling price at 30%, 40% and 50% profit margin
4. What my cost per piece becomes if flour goes up by ₦10,000 a bag
5. Three options if costs rise: raise the price, reduce the size, or adjust the recipe, with pros and cons
Show your working so I can check it with a calculator.

The allergen label

The UK standard

Since 2021, Natasha's Law requires food packed on site to carry a full ingredient list with the 14 major allergens highlighted in bold. It applies to a one-person stall the same as to a supermarket.

The AI shortcut

Paste the recipe from Win 1. AI writes the ingredient list in the right order, bolds the allergens, and adds storage and best-before lines.

This isn't only a UK idea. NAFDAC's Bread Regulations also require allergenic ingredients to be named on the label, yet a 2025 study found many Nigerian packaged foods still fall short. A clear label makes a small bakery look like a brand, and it's the first thing a supermarket buyer checks.

Prompt to copy 
Using the recipe above, write a product label for my [product] in the style required by UK Natasha's Law. Include:
- Product name
- Full ingredients list in descending order of weight, with any of the 14 major allergens in CAPITALS or bold
- A "may contain" line if my kitchen also handles [nuts / sesame / eggs, etc.]
- Net weight
- Best before: [number] days from baking. Storage instructions.
- Batch number format I can write by hand
- My business name, address and phone: [details]
Keep it short enough to fit a small sticker.
Soft Milk Bread · 500g Ingredients: WHEAT flour, water, sugar, vegetable fat, MILK powder, yeast, salt, flour improver. May contain: EGGS, SESAME.
Best before: 3 daysBatch: 27/09-A
Store in a cool, dry place. Example layout only.
They leave withLabel text for their bestseller, ready to print on stickers at any business centre.

Say clearly: this label raises their standard, but packaged products sold in shops still need NAFDAC registration. Point them to NAFDAC for their specific product.

The food-safety diary

The UK standard

The Food Standards Agency gives small food businesses a free pack called Safer Food, Better Business: a daily diary of opening checks, closing checks, cleaning and temperatures. Inspectors award a 0–5 hygiene rating that goes on the door. Without a written system, a business can't score above 1.

The AI shortcut

Describe the bakery by voice note. AI writes a one-page daily checklist and cleaning schedule sized for a Nigerian bakery.
Prompt to copy 
I run a bakery in [city], Nigeria with [number] staff. We make [products] using [oven type, mixer, etc.]. We have / don't have constant power and a fridge.

Based on the UK Food Standards Agency's "Safer Food, Better Business" approach (the 4 Cs: cleaning, cooking, chilling, cross-contamination), create:
1. A daily OPENING checklist (tick boxes)
2. A daily CLOSING checklist (tick boxes)
3. A weekly cleaning schedule table: what, how often, who
4. A simple rule card for staff on handwashing, hair covering, illness and allergens
5. A short WhatsApp Status post telling customers about our hygiene routine
Keep sentences short. It must fit on one page for the wall.
They leave withA one-page wall checklist, a cleaning rota, and a post that tells customers why their bakery is safer.

Make the point: in the UK customers choose the bakery with the 5-star sticker. Nigerian customers are no different. Showing the routine sells.

Reviews and reputation

The UK standard

A UK bakery lives on its Google reviews. Good owners ask every happy customer for one and reply to every review, good or bad, usually within a day.

The AI shortcut

Set up a free Google Business Profile so they appear on Google Maps. Then AI writes the "please review us" message and drafts calm replies to any review.
97%of consumers read reviews before choosing a local business (BrightLocal 2026)
88% vs 47%would use a business that replies to all reviews, against one that replies to none
Prompt A · asking for reviews 
Write a short, warm WhatsApp message I can send the day after a customer collects their order, thanking them and asking them to leave a Google review at [link]. Make it one version in English and one in Pidgin. No pressure, no bribes.
Prompt B · replying to a bad review 
A customer left this review: "[paste review]". Write a calm, professional public reply, the way a good UK bakery would. Thank them, don't argue, take responsibility where it's fair, and invite them to message me on [number] to put it right. Under 80 words.
They leave withA Google Business Profile started, a review-request message, and a template for handling complaints in public.

Google Business Profile is free and works in Nigeria. Verification can take a few days, so they start it in the room and finish it at home.

The zero-waste close

The UK standard

Greggs sells end-of-day surplus as "Magic Bags" through Too Good To Go at about a third of the price, and passed six million bags in 2026. UK bakeries also use AI forecasting: Foodforecast reports about 30% fewer unsold returns.

The AI shortcut

Too Good To Go isn't in Nigeria, but WhatsApp Status is. AI writes a daily "surprise bag" offer, and a simple notebook lets AI forecast how much to bake.
Prompt A · the surprise bag 
At the end of each day I have leftover [bread, buns, cakes]. Help me copy the UK "Too Good To Go" idea using WhatsApp. Suggest:
1. A name for my end-of-day surprise bag
2. What goes in it and a price (about a third of normal)
3. Three WhatsApp Status messages to post at [time] each day
4. Simple rules so it doesn't hurt my full-price sales
Prompt B · after two weeks of notes 
Here are my last 14 days: [day – baked – sold – left over]. Which days am I over-baking or running out? How many should I bake each day of the week next week? Explain it simply.
They leave withA named surplus-bag offer to post tonight, and a two-week notebook habit that lets AI cut their waste.
Take-home extras

Six more UK habits for the handout

These don't fit in the 60 minutes, but they're easy to follow alone at home. Put them on the back of the handout.

Written order terms

UK cake makers put deposits, cancellation and allergy rules in writing before they bake. AI writes them and a "reply CONFIRM" message.

Prompt 
Write simple terms and conditions for my custom cake orders, like a UK cake business would: deposit of [50%], notice needed, cancellation and refund rules, allergy responsibility, delivery. Then write an order summary message the customer must reply "CONFIRM" to.

A WhatsApp shop that answers itself

The free WhatsApp Business app can send a greeting, an away message and quick replies like /price. AI writes them all.

Prompt 
I run [bakery] in [area]. I sell [products with prices], deliver to [areas] for ₦___, open [hours]. Write my WhatsApp Business greeting message, away message, and quick replies for /menu /price /order /deposit /delivery /thanks.

A photo coach, not a fake cake

AI reviews their product photo and gives three phone-only tips. ChatGPT's voice mode understands Pidgin, so they can describe the cake out loud and get captions back.

Prompt 
This is a photo of my [product]. Give me 3 ways to make my next photo better using only my phone: light, angle, background. Then write 3 captions, one in Pidgin, ending with "Order on WhatsApp: [number]".

Customer service practice

AI plays a customer who asks for "last price" or complains about a late cake, then scores the reply. It's good training for new counter staff.

Prompt 
Role-play a Nigerian customer who wants a big discount on a birthday cake and wants to pay after delivery. One message at a time. After 6 messages, score me out of 10 on holding my price, getting a deposit and staying friendly.

A 90-day selling calendar

UK bakeries plan seasonal ranges months ahead. AI lists every occasion coming up and the day to start promoting it.

Prompt 
Today is [date]. I sell [products] in [city], Nigeria. Make a 90-day table: Occasion | Date | Product to push | Day to start promoting | Post idea. Include public holidays, festivals, school dates, weddings and office parties.

A training card for every new staff member

UK bakeries induct staff with written steps. They describe a process by voice note and AI turns it into a one-page card.

Prompt 
I'll describe how we make [product]. Turn it into a one-page step-by-step training card for a new apprentice, including target weights and times, and the hygiene rules they must follow.

Golden rules

Five rules to say out loud before they leave

Check the mathsAI makes arithmetic mistakes. Ask it to show its working, then check with a calculator.
AI drafts, the law decidesLabels and hygiene checklists raise your standard. For NAFDAC registration, follow NAFDAC's own rules.
Show the standardPut the label on the pack, the checklist on the wall and the review link by the till. Customers trust what they can see.
Protect customer detailsDon't paste customers' phone numbers, addresses or bank details into AI.
Never fake the cakeImprove the photo, not the product. Only post what you actually baked.
