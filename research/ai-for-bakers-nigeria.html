Oven to Phone: how UK small businesses use AI, put to work in a Nigerian bakery

A baker in Lagos and a shop owner in London have the same AI tools on their phones. The difference is the habits. More UK small businesses now treat AI as a trained assistant built into their daily routine, rather than somewhere to ask the odd question. This session teaches five of those habits, each practised on a real bakery task.

The one skill under all five

The four-part prompt

Most weak AI answers come from weak questions. UK AI trainers teach a structure like this one. Teach it once, then point back to it in every win.

1 · WhoWho you are"I run a small bakery in Ibadan with 3 staff."
2 · WhatWhat you need"Work out my cost per loaf."
3 · DetailsThe facts it can't guessRecipe, prices, customers, deadlines, rules.
4 · ShapeHow the answer should look"A table. Show your working. Under 100 words. In Pidgin."
Weak prompt
How much should I sell my bread?
Four-part prompt
I run a small bakery in Ibadan (who). Work out the right price for my 500g loaf (what). Recipe and what I paid for each ingredient: [...] (details). Give me a table, show your working, and give prices at 30% and 40% margin (shape).

The AI task audit

How UK small businesses do it

UK AI consultants start with a list of every repeated task in the week, then sort it: what AI can draft, what it can help decide, and what stays human. UK owners still lose about 8 hours a week to admin, and nearly half have never tried to automate any of it.

In a Nigerian bakery

The baker lists a normal week: answering price DMs, writing orders down, costing, posting, briefing staff, chasing deposits. AI names the three tasks to hand over first.
Prompt to copy 
I run a bakery in [city], Nigeria with [number] staff. Here is everything I do in a normal week, and roughly how long each takes:
[e.g. answering WhatsApp price questions – 1 hr a day; writing down orders; working out prices; posting on Status/Instagram; briefing staff; chasing deposits; buying ingredients]

Sort these into three groups:
1. AI can draft it for me
2. AI can help me decide
3. Must stay with me or my staff
Then pick the 3 tasks I should hand to AI first, with how many hours a week each could save. Give me a table.
They leave withTheir own top three tasks to hand to AI, with an estimate of hours saved. The rest of the session works on those tasks.

Your bakery's own AI assistant

How UK small businesses do it

Instead of re-explaining the business in every chat, UK firms set up a custom assistant once, with their products, prices, policies and tone of voice. Every answer after that comes out in their style and with their facts.

In a Nigerian bakery

Google's Gemini Gems are free to build. The baker pastes in the template below, and from then on AI answers like a staff member who knows the menu, the prices and the deposit rule.

Steps

  1. Open gemini.google.com in the phone's browser and sign in with a Google account.
  2. Choose Gems → New Gem. Name it after the bakery.
  3. Paste the template, fill the brackets, save.
  4. Test it with: "A customer wants a 2-step cake for Saturday. What do I reply?"

No Google account?

  • In ChatGPT, go to Settings → Personalisation → Custom instructions and paste a shorter version. Free accounts get about 1,500 characters.
Assistant instructions to copy 
You are the assistant for [bakery name], a bakery in [area, city], Nigeria, run by [owner name].

What we sell and prices: [product – price, one per line]
Delivery: [areas] for ₦___. Opening hours: [hours].
Custom cakes: [x] days' notice, [50%] deposit before baking, no refund after baking starts.
Payment: [transfer / POS / cash].

How to write: warm, respectful and short, like a friendly Nigerian shop owner. Use English unless I ask for Pidgin.
Rules:
- Never invent a price or a promise. If a detail is missing, ask me.
- For any money calculation, show your working.
- Customer messages should end by asking for the order details or the deposit.
They leave withAn assistant on their phone that already knows their bakery. They can hand it to a staff member or use it for every task on the page.

This is the habit that makes the biggest difference. A one-off chat forgets everything, but a set-up assistant gets more useful every week. Remind them to update the prices in the Gem whenever prices change.

Snap it, don't type it

How UK small businesses do it

UK firms photograph receipts, invoices and handwritten notes and let software read them into tables, rather than typing them up. Research and summarising is the biggest time-saver UK SMEs report.

In a Nigerian bakery

Photograph the recipe notebook and the last market receipt. AI reads both and works out the real cost per loaf, then what happens when flour goes up again.

Why it lands: a 50kg bag of flour has moved between roughly ₦55,000 and ₦75,000, and yeast and butter have more than doubled since 2023. Many bakers still price by feel. Seeing their own cost per loaf worked out from a photo gets the room's attention.

Prompt to copy · attach both photos 
Photo 1 is my recipe for [product]. It makes [number] pieces. Photo 2 is my latest receipt for ingredients.
Other costs per batch: gas/diesel/power ₦___, packaging ₦___ per piece, labour ₦___.

1. Read both photos and put the ingredients, quantities and prices in a table. Ask me if anything is unclear.
2. Work out the cost of ONE piece.
3. Give selling prices at 30%, 40% and 50% profit margin.
4. Show what happens to my cost per piece if flour goes up by ₦10,000 a bag.
Show your working so I can check it with a calculator.

Facilitator note: AI can misread handwriting and make arithmetic mistakes. That's why the prompt asks it to put the figures in a table first and show its working. Tell them to check both.

Talk it, don't type it

How UK small businesses do it

UK owners dictate emails, record meetings and let AI write up the notes. Emails and business messages are the second-biggest time-saver UK SMEs report.

In a Nigerian bakery

Bakers' hands are in dough, so talking suits them better than typing. ChatGPT's voice mode understands Nigerian Pidgin. A quick spoken note after a phone order becomes a clean written confirmation.
Prompt A · spoken order to written confirmation 
I'm going to describe a cake order I just took on the phone. Turn it into:
1. A clear order summary for the customer to reply "CONFIRM" to, including date, size, flavour, the exact spelling of any name on the cake, delivery or pickup, price and deposit
2. A short production note for my staff
If I forgot any detail, list it at the end so I can ask the customer.
Prompt B · spoken process to a staff card 
I'll describe how we make [product] out loud. Turn it into a one-page step-by-step card for a new apprentice, with target weights, times and temperatures, and a short hygiene checklist at the bottom. Use short, simple sentences.
They leave withAn order-confirmation habit that stops wrong-name cakes and no-shows, and a way to write staff instructions without writing.

Let your numbers talk

How UK small businesses do it

UK firms paste their sales figures into AI and ask what the numbers show. UK bakeries use AI forecasting to decide how much to bake; one provider, Foodforecast, reports about 30% fewer unsold returns.

In a Nigerian bakery

No software needed. A notebook with baked, sold and left over each day for two weeks is enough data for AI to spot the patterns.
Prompt to copy · after two weeks of notes 
Here are my last 14 days for each product: [day – product – baked – sold – left over].

1. Which days am I over-baking, and which days am I running out?
2. How many of each should I bake on each day of the week next week?
3. Which product earns me the most, and which should I think about dropping?
4. Suggest one way to sell leftovers at the end of the day without hurting full-price sales.
Explain it simply and show a table.
They leave withA two-week notebook template to start tomorrow, and a prompt that turns it into a baking plan.

In the room, use a made-up two-week list on the projector so they can see the output now. Their own numbers come in two weeks, which is also a good reason for a follow-up WhatsApp group.

Take-home extras

Six more habits for the handout

Each one is another way UK small businesses work with AI, shown on a bakery task. Put them on the back of the handout.

Keep a prompt library

UK teams save prompts that work and reuse them. On WhatsApp, "Message yourself" makes a free prompt library: save every prompt from this session there.

Prompt 
Here's a prompt that worked well for me: [paste]. Turn it into a reusable template with [brackets] for the parts I'll change each time.

Draft first, you approve

UK businesses let AI draft customer replies but a person checks and sends them. Use this to fill the WhatsApp Business greeting, away message and quick replies.

Prompt 
Using what you know about my bakery, write my WhatsApp Business greeting message, away message, and quick replies for /menu /price /order /deposit /delivery /thanks.

Rehearse the hard conversations

UK sales teams practise with AI before difficult calls. AI plays the customer who wants "last price" and then scores the reply.

Prompt 
Role-play a Nigerian customer who wants a big discount on a birthday cake and wants to pay after delivery. One message at a time. After 6 messages, score me out of 10 on holding my price, getting a deposit and staying friendly.

Get a second opinion

For big decisions, careful UK users ask two different AIs and compare. Try ChatGPT and Gemini on a price rise or a new oven purchase.

Prompt 
I'm deciding whether to [raise my bread price by ₦___ / buy a new oven for ₦___]. Give me the strongest case for, the strongest case against, and 3 questions I should answer before I decide.

Plan the season ahead

AI lists every occasion coming up with a product to push and the day to start promoting it, so they can buy flour and packaging early.

Prompt 
Today is [date]. I sell [products] in [city], Nigeria. Make a 90-day table: Occasion | Date | Product to push | Day to start promoting | Post idea. Include public holidays, festivals, school dates, weddings and office parties.

A photo coach, not a fake cake

AI reviews a product photo and gives phone-only tips, then writes captions. It improves the photo, never the cake.

Prompt 
This is a photo of my [product]. Give me 3 ways to make my next photo better using only my phone: light, angle, background. Then write 3 captions, one in Pidgin, ending with "Order on WhatsApp: [number]".

Golden rules

Five rules to say out loud before they leave

AI drafts, you decideRead everything before it goes to a customer. The name on the message is yours.
Check the mathsAsk AI to show its working, then check it with a calculator.
Protect customer detailsNigeria's Data Protection Act 2023 covers customer data. Don't paste phone numbers, addresses or bank details into AI.
Never fake the cakeImprove the photo, not the product. Only post what you actually baked.
One habit at a timePick one habit and use it daily for two weeks before adding the next.
