Where the money is for functional masterbatch

The headline for the meeting

Masterbatch is capital-heavy and margin-thin, so venture capital mostly ignores it. The money that fits us is government incentives and cheap loans (strongest in Malaysia), customers and brand owners paying for recyclability (strongest in the UK), and trade buyers or private equity consolidating specialists. Regulation is what pushes that money toward functional grades right now.

What this means for us: every brand owner selling into the UK now pays more for packaging that is hard to recycle or low in recycled content. Grades that fix that problem (compatibilisers for recycled content, NIR-detectable blacks, stabilisers that let recyclate perform, clean-label additives) have a buyer with a budget. Malaysia is two to four years behind on the same curve, which is a chance to be the local supplier before EPR becomes mandatory.

The market

Big players and where they get their money

Global majors

Avient (formerly PolyOne, bought Clariant's masterbatch business in 2020), Ampacet, LyondellBasell (A. Schulman), Cabot, Tosaf, Plastika Kritis.

Funded from their own balance sheets, bond markets and listed equity. They grow by acquisition, which makes them potential buyers, partners or distributors of a specialist.

UK specialists

Hubron (black masterbatch, Manchester area), Broadway Colours (colour, additive, and certified graphene masterbatch compounding partner for HydroGraph), Colloids (Tosaf-owned since 2004).

Mostly privately owned. Pattern: owner-managed firms that end up sold to a major or to private equity.

Malaysia

DIC Compounds Malaysia (Japanese-owned), Perfect Colourants & Plastics (one of the largest local producers), plus a long tail of private SMEs in Johor, Selangor and Penang.

Local firms lean on bank debt, MIDF soft loans and MIDA tax incentives. Few listed pure plays on Bursa, so equity comes mostly from family and strategic owners.

Recent product moves show where the majors are spending: Ampacet launched an NIR-detectable black for recyclable food packaging (Oct 2025), and Tosaf widened its compostable range in 2026. Those are the functional niches with the clearest buyers.

What each team does

How we go after it

Finance who pays

  • Malaysia: check our score under MIDA's New Incentive Framework (live since 1 Mar 2026; up to 100% Investment Tax Allowance or special tax rates as low as 0%, for up to 15 years).
  • Malaysia: price new lines through MIDF's automation soft loan (up to RM20m, 4% for SMEs) and GTFS 5.0 guarantees (60–80% of the green portion, open to 31 Dec 2026).
  • UK: claim R&D tax relief on formulation work; apply for Made Smarter (up to £20k matched) for line digitisation.

Marketing who buys

  • Lead with the customer's cost, not our chemistry: "this grade moves your pack from red to green under EPR" or "helps you pass 30% recycled content".
  • Target UK converters and brand owners with heavy plastic packaging tax bills first; they have budget.
  • In Malaysia, join the MPMA voluntary-EPR effort to be visible before 2030.

Technical what we prove

  • Pick one or two hero grades with measurable proof: recyclate performance, NIR sortability, RAM recyclability rating.
  • Build case-study data with a customer trial; this is what grants and buyers both ask for.
  • Scan open Innovate UK competitions (Smart grants are paused; themed calls are open, often needing a partner).

Operations where we make it

  • Use Malaysia for cost-efficient capacity backed by incentives; the UK for technical service and close customer work.
  • Document energy and waste savings on any new line; green financing depends on them.
  • Keep traceability records now; the UK plans mandatory certification of recycled content claims.

Analysis

What John built

A capital-flow intelligence dashboard: a map that layers who holds money (billionaires by wealth tier, banks, VCs, family offices), where it sits geographically, which sectors it goes into, and which companies receive it. The heat map shows concentration; zooming into a region (Africa, London) shows the sectors and deals underneath. His angle was a gap lens: seeing how little of that flow reaches Black-led businesses.

1 · COLLECT

Wealth lists (e.g. Forbes), deal databases (Crunchbase, PitchBook), company registries (Companies House), filings and news.

2 · CONNECT

MCP servers

MCP (Model Context Protocol) lets an AI assistant call those APIs as tools, so it can pull and join data by asking questions.

3 · MAP

Geo heat layers

Records tagged with location, sector and amount, drawn with a map library (Leaflet, Mapbox or deck.gl).

4 · HOST

Railway

Railway is a hosting service: it runs the app and database online. It is where the app lives, not the tool that built it.

Your security question

Is it legal to show billionaires' names?

Mostly fine

Net worth estimates, company holdings and published investments are public and widely republished. Using them for analysis is normal practice.

Watch for

"Public" is not a free pass. Under UK GDPR, individuals' data still needs a lawful purpose, even when it is online. Many data providers' terms forbid scraping or republishing. Home addresses or family details would cross the line.
