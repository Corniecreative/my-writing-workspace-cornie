# Stop selling mixers, start equipping food businesses

Your sales are slow mainly because of how your model is built, not because of you. You sell an expensive, occasional, easy-to-price-check item (a ₦145k–₦280k stand mixer) one at a time, to a buyer whose own customers are cutting back on celebration cakes. On top of that, free shipping eats your margin and the 3-unit cap leaves resellers nothing worth committing to. The market is moving toward food people buy every day, like small chops, shawarma, street meals and event catering, and toward equipment that earns its owner money. Household buying intentions for appliances, meanwhile, are deep in negative territory. The best way out of the "cake box" is to stay with the same type of customer (people who make food to sell) and widen what you sell them. I suggest two lines. The first is food-vendor equipment (fryers, food warmers, griddles, commercial blenders), sold as starter kits. The second is fast-moving baking and food-business consumables and small tools, which give resellers something that sells every week. China should stay your main source because it is roughly 2–2.5× cheaper per unit and lets you reorder the identical item. UK clearance stock is worth testing only as a small premium side line of tested, branded pieces. Resellers will work once they hold no stock, sell low-ticket items that move weekly, and earn on clear price tiers. The 90-day plan at the end shows how to test all of this cheaply before you commit real money.

## Your current model sells a rare purchase to a squeezed buyer, then pays to deliver it

Start with what a stand mixer is to your customer: a one-time purchase in a crowded, transparent market. On Jiji, a Sokany 5.5L mixer is listed at **₦145,000** and a Progress 2-in-1 mixer-blender at **₦280,000** ([Jiji Sokany](https://jiji.ng/ikeja/kitchen-appliances/sokany-stand-mixer-5-5liters-uplE2Gb54GtUwFNNzWNtQ3Vd.html); [Jiji Progress](https://jiji.ng/ha/surulere/kitchen-appliances/progress-2-in-1-stand-mixer-and-blender-l8lh3lTAcfzaH7ENZSepDNsP.html)), and a formal online store sells a Rohnson 7L at **₦180,000** ([Zit.ng](https://zit.ng/products/rohnson-stand-mixer)). Any buyer can check your price against these in thirty seconds. A baker buys one mixer, maybe two, and then not again for years. So each sale means finding a brand-new customer, which is why sales feel slow even when you do everything right.

The buyer is also under pressure. Bakers were still paying **₦55,000–₦62,000 per 50kg bag of flour** in December 2025, and their association called claimed price cuts "a drop in the ocean" ([BusinessDay](https://businessday.ng/agriculture/article/bakers-dismiss-flour-price-reduction-claim/)). Bakers have reported customers dropping children's birthday cakes for biscuits and drinks ([Guardian NG](https://guardian.ng/news/nigeria/bakers-decry-low-patronage-of-pastries-amidst-high-cost-of-materials/)); that report is probably from 2024, so treat it as a signal rather than current data. I found no hard figures on whether home baking is saturated. It is plausible, but not proven.

Your other choices make it harder. **The 3-unit cap** stops any reseller from earning a volume price. Even at the cap, they would tie up roughly ₦450k–₦850k in a slow item their customers can price-check on Jiji, which is the classic reason reseller schemes stall. **Free shipping** is costly. Interstate courier fees for parcels of 5kg and up run about **₦12,000–₦18,000+** from Lagos to Abuja, plus 10–25% for re-weighing and handling ([brands.ng](https://brands.ng/blog/gig-logistics-price-list-2026-what-it-really-costs-to-ship-in-nigeria/)). These are third-party estimates and they conflict with each other. A boxed mixer probably weighs 6–9kg (my estimate), so one "free" interstate delivery can cost about as much as a whole ₦20,000 broker fee. **Your ₦20,000 broker fee** is a flat amount on equipment that probably costs hundreds of thousands to millions of naira, so you are likely earning a very small percentage. I could not source Nigerian deck-oven prices, so check your own recent deals. Your **locally made baking pans** are the one part of the business that already behaves the right way: cheap, needed again and again, and easy for someone else to resell.

What you have built is useful: supplier relationships in China, a list of baker customers, relationships with oven and table dealers, and a habit of personal service. The problem is the shape of the offer, and you can change that.

## The market is moving toward everyday food and equipment that earns money

The big picture improved in 2026, but households are still squeezed. Headline inflation fell to **15.39% in August 2026**, from 23.14% a year earlier. Food inflation is still **19.57%** ([VON](https://von.gov.ng/nigerias-headline-inflation-eases-to-15-39-in-august-nbs/)). The naira held around **₦1,370/$** in May 2026, about 13.5% stronger year on year ([Nairametrics](https://nm-stag.nairametrics.com/naira-weakens-against-dollar-closes-may-at-n1372/)). The official rate was about **₦1,331.50/$ on 7 October 2026** ([TechEconomy](https://techeconomy.ng/dollar-to-naira-naira-slips-to-133290-at-official-market-strengthens-to-1357-parallel)). The CBN cut its policy rate to **23%** in September 2026 ([Nairametrics](https://nairametrics.com/2026/09/22/cbn-resets-mpr-to-23/)). For an importer, this is the steadiest period since 2023: landed costs are more predictable and the risk of having to reprice stock is lower.

Households tell a different story. The CBN's consumer sentiment index fell to **–18.7 in September 2026**, and its buying-intention index was **16.6, far below the neutral 50** ([BrandSpur](https://brandspurng.com/2026/10/05/nigerias-consumer-confidence-falls-to-18-7-points-as-inflation-fears-deepen/)). Earlier CBN surveys showed especially negative intentions for appliances ([Nairametrics](https://nairametrics.com/2026/08/07/nigerians-suspend-home-car-purchases-as-living-costs-squeeze-households/)), and BCG found **83% of Nigerian households cutting discretionary purchases** ([BusinessDay](https://businessday.ng/?p=1066360)). In practice, a home baker treating themselves to a first mixer is a hard sale right now. A vendor buying a fryer that will make money from next week is easier, because the purchase pays for itself.

Demand is holding up where food is cheap and eaten often. Small chops vendors say they stay profitable through price rises "because these snacks are affordable", and small chops are standard at weddings, birthdays and corporate events ([Leadership](https://leadership.ng/?p=633623)). Lagos street food has been reported to "defy the cash crunch", and newcomers, including unemployed graduates, keep entering it ([Daily Trust](https://dailytrust.com/how-street-food-business-defies-cash-crunch-in-lagos/)). That source dates from about 2023 and no one has measured these segments, but the direction is consistent. The equipment these vendors buy also shows up as a bundle. One import request for delivery to Lagos lists an **electric fryer, electric griddle, bain-marie (food warmer) and gas and electric shawarma machines** together, 220–240V ([Freshdi](https://freshdi.com/request/Cooking-Hot-Food-Equipment)). Shawarma machines sell for roughly **₦150k–₦350k** for standard units ([Nigerian Queries](https://nigerianqueries.com/sharwama-machine-prices-in-nigeria/)). Local fabricators already make stainless gas shawarma grills ([Jiji](https://jiji.ng/mushin/restaurant-and-catering-equipment/fryer-with-foreign-burner-e1vrjCXQZ6b9KKLJWxkkTU2W.html)), so your imported advantage is on items that are hard to make well locally, such as electric fryers with thermostats, food warmers and commercial blenders. Cooking gas is getting dearer: a 12.5kg refill averaged **₦22,382 in April 2026**, up 10.4% year on year ([Gazette NG](https://gazettengr.com/tinubu-hardship-prices-of-cooking-gas-continue-to-rise-in-nigeria/)). That makes "uses less gas" or electric options a real selling point.

In household kitchenware, the volume is in **blenders**, with **air fryers** as the growth niche. Euromonitor names influencer marketing as a driver of blender demand and says offline shops still dominate ([Euromonitor](https://www.euromonitor.com/consumer-appliances-in-nigeria/report)). Air fryers drive "modest growth amid economic constraints" ([Euromonitor](https://www.euromonitor.com/small-cooking-appliances-in-nigeria/report)). Both categories are crowded, though: Jiji carries about **7,171 blender ads and 5,142 air-fryer ads** ([Jiji](https://jiji.ng/kitchen-appliances/nikai)). Pots are a weak bet for you. Formal makers like Tower Aluminium compete there, along with artisans who cast recycled-aluminium pots cheaply for weddings and parties ([Odua Investment](https://oduainvestment.com.ng/tower-aluminium-nigeria-limited); [PIND](https://pindfoundation.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/06/Recycling-Strategy-Brief.pdf)). **Food flasks** are an interesting outlier: Lagos Island sellers market them for events, church programmes, caterers and souvenirs ([Jiji](https://jiji.ng/ha/lagos-island-west/kitchen-and-dining/2l-food-flask-BXfppilcoAXQqmTpbanBFBek.html)), which hints at bulk orders. No one has sized that market.

Duty may also favour commercial equipment. Nigeria uses the ECOWAS tariff bands, with **5% for capital goods and 20% for consumer goods**, plus 7.5% VAT and smaller levies ([trade.gov](https://www.trade.gov/knowledge-product/nigeria-import-tariffs); [DHL Nigeria](https://www.dhl.com/discover/en-ng/logistics-advice/essential-guides/importing-to-nigeria-)). Some commercial food equipment may fall in a lower band than household appliances. That is not confirmed. **Verify the exact rates with a licensed customs broker** for HS 8509 (mixers/blenders), 8516 (fryers, ovens), 8419 (some commercial cooking equipment), 7323 (steel cookware) and 7615 (aluminium cookware). The 4% FOB levy was suspended in September 2025 ([AllAfrica](https://allafrica.com/stories/202509160081.html)), and I found no sign it has come back. Confirm that too before you price a shipment.

## Two product lines that widen your crowd without leaving it

The test for a new line is simple. Does it sell to people like your current buyers (small food businesses), does it sell more often than a mixer, and can a reseller move it without tying up much cash? Scored against those three questions, the options sort out clearly.

| Candidate line | Same/similar crowd? | Buys repeat? | Reseller-friendly? | Competition | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Food-vendor equipment (electric/gas fryers, bain-marie/food warmers, griddles, commercial blenders) | Yes: caterers, small chops, shawarma, bakers who also do snacks | Medium: vendors upgrade and add units | Yes, as pre-order/no-stock catalogue items | Local fabricators on some items | **Pick #1** |
| Baking and food-business consumables and smallware (cake boards, boxes, food packs, piping bags, nozzles, turntables, your pans) | Yes: every baker and caterer | High: weekly | Very: low ticket, fast | Many local suppliers | **Pick #2** |
| Household blenders | Broader household crowd | Low | Medium | Very crowded | Maybe later, a commercial blender only |
| Air fryers | Household | Low | Medium | Crowded; recall risk on some UK models | Skip for now |
| Pots and cookware sets | Household and caterers | Low | Medium | Strong local and artisanal | Skip, except large catering pots as a kit add-on |
| Food flasks / event coolers | Caterers, events, souvenirs | Seasonal bulk | Good for bulk | Lagos Island cluster | Optional test inside Pick #2 |

**Pick #1, food-vendor equipment sold as starter kits,** moves you from "cake equipment seller" to "the person who equips food businesses". Your brokered deck ovens and stainless tables already belong in this world, so the line ties your scattered offers into one story. Sell kits, not single machines. A "small chops starter" (fryer, food warmer, work table, large bowls) and a "baker's graduate kit" (mixer, pans, turntable, nozzles, first consumables) are the kind of thing that sells. Kitchen Realm, which claims 5,000+ Nigerian bakers served, sells baking starter bundles at around **₦70,000–₦95,000 with free delivery**, marketed as "starting or scaling a baking business" ([Kitchen Realm Store](https://kitchenrealmstore.carrd.co/)). That claim is unverified marketing, and the page was read only as a search snippet. The idea is the same one you would use: sell the outcome, and the bundle price is high enough to cover delivery. Government and corporate programmes also buy kits like these in bulk. NSIPA gave starter packs to **18,000 vocational graduates**, with catering among the trades ([MSME Africa](https://msmeafricaonline.com/federal-government-distributes-starter-packs-to-18000-vocational-training-beneficiaries/)), and SMEDAN equips trained entrepreneurs state by state ([MSME Africa](https://msmeafricaonline.com/smedan-to-equip-in-six-states/)).

**Pick #2, consumables and smallware,** fixes the one-sale-and-gone problem. A baker buys a mixer once but buys boards, boxes and piping bags every week. The bakery market is projected to grow about **8.5% a year from 2026 to 2032** ([Nexdigm](https://www.nexdigm.com/market-research/insights/blog/nigeria-bakery-products-industry/)). Local carton suppliers already sell cake and food boxes wholesale through WhatsApp storefronts ([Take.app CartonWarehouseNG](https://take.app/cartonwarehouseng)), so you can test demand by buying locally before you import anything. This is also the line that will finally make resellers worth recruiting. Your locally made pans fit here, and spare parts for the mixers you have sold (bowls, beaters, dough hooks) also fit. Spares are something the anonymous Jiji sellers cannot offer.

## China stays your engine; UK clearance is a side dish, not a new menu

On cost per unit and the ability to reorder the same item, China wins clearly. Factory air fryers cost about **US$21–37 FOB** and home stand mixers about **US$39–65 FOB** on export platforms, with minimum orders of 500+ ([Made-in-China](https://www.made-in-china.com/products-search/hot-china-products/Wholesale_Stand_Mixer.html)). 1688 prices through a buying agent are lower, at about **¥95–218** for air fryers ([1688](https://wiki.1688.com/en/kitchen-appliances/WKfxunsiyw4lxc)), and agents let you mix small quantities. Most 1688 sellers won't take Nigerian cards, so buyers usually pay through a local procurement agent ([Connectciti](https://connectciti.com/blog/ultimate-guide-to-mini-importation-from-china-to-nigeria)). Shared-container (LCL) sea freight from China to Lagos is mostly **US$85–195 per cubic metre** in 2026 ([Winsail](https://winsaillogistics.com/shipping-cost-per-cbm-from-china-to-nigeria/); [Goodhope](https://goodhopefreight.com/nigeria/lcl.html)), and a full 20ft container costs **US$2,970–3,630** ([Sino-Shipping](https://www.sino-shipping.com/freight-china-nigeria/)).

UK diaspora cargo firms charge **by weight, about £2.00–£3.50/kg** by sea, with 6–8 week transit ([CargoAfrik](https://cargoafrik.co.uk/price-list-of-cargo-to-nigeria/); [A-B Cargo Express](http://www.abcargoexpress.com/a-bce/); [Hezelinks](https://hezelinks.com/sea-freight-cargo-shipping-from-uk-to-nigeria/)). That is hard on heavy kitchen goods. UK clearance pricing has tiers. Retailer auctions (B-Stock and similar) can go as low as 8–15% of retail price, but those figures are from the US and the auctions favour registered bulk buyers ([Closo](https://closo.co/blogs/inventory-liquidation/are-amazon-pallets-a-real-thing)). Smaller buyers usually pay resellers more. One lot of grade-B kitchen-appliance returns sold for about **43% of retail price** ([Stocklear](https://joblot.stocklear.co.uk/auction/473)). Here is a rough worked example for one mid-range air fryer. Every input is flagged, and the GBP rate of about ₦1,770/£ is an unsourced assumption.

| Step (one air fryer, ~5.5kg) | UK grade-B return | China new (1688 via agent) |
|---|---|---|
| Buy price | ~£28 (40% of an assumed £70 retail price) ≈ ₦49.6k | ~¥113 ≈ $16 ≈ ₦21k (agent fee extra) |
| Sea freight | £14–19 ≈ ₦24k–34k (per kg) | $5–10 ≈ ₦7k–13k (per CBM) |
| Duty, VAT, levies (*assumes the 20% band, ~31% of CIF; verify*) | ≈ ₦23k–27k | ≈ ₦9k–11k |
| SONCAP | $350+ per shipment, a heavy cost for a small mixed lot | Spread over many identical units |
| Defect write-off | Assume 10–25% for returns (*unsourced*) | Lower, with inspection |
| **Rough landed cost** | **≈ ₦100k–120k per working unit** | **≈ ₦40k–50k** |

Three more facts settle the comparison. First, the plugs are no problem. Nigeria runs on **230V/50Hz with the UK Type G plug as the official standard** ([WorldStandards](https://www.worldstandards.eu/electricity/plug-voltage-by-country/nigeria/)). But Chinese factories will fit UK plugs on request, so this doesn't tip the balance. Second, **SONCAP certification** applies to electricals. It costs a $500–$2,000 Product Certificate plus **$350 per shipment** ([Bureau Veritas](https://cdn2-verigates.bureauveritas.com/sites/verigates/files/2024-10/NIGERIA%20%28SONCAP%29%20Rev%208.4.pdf)), and goods arriving without it can be held or destroyed at your cost ([SON](https://son.gov.ng/helpie_faq/why-do-i-need-soncap-certificate-for-my-import-to-nigeria/)). SGS's older 2020 fee sheet shows lower fees, so confirm the current amounts. That fixed fee hurts small, mixed UK lots far more than repeat China orders of one model. Third, **untested UK returns carry legal risk**. Importing non-working electricals counts as e-waste, with fines up to **₦5m or two years' imprisonment**, and about a quarter of studied used imports didn't work ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/features/2026/3/27/truly-junk-e-waste-from-rich-nations-floods-local-markets-in-nigeria)). Some UK lots also contain recalled Tower air fryers ([UK Gov recall](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/68387e1bc99c4f37ab4e86a5/2503-0094-product-recall-tower-air-fryer.pdf)). Treat UK stock as a side line, and only for tested, working units.

UK stock still has a real place, because Nigerian buyers pay more for "original" and "UK used". UK-used cookers, microwaves and fridges are listed at about **₦25,000–₦370,000** ([Jiji](https://jiji.ng/ibadan/kitchen-appliances/tokunbo-imported-uk-used-table-gas-cooker-8Qh8AuLgo3NZ6LMMeJ68tKhu.html)). The sensible use is a small premium line: tested, branded, higher-value pieces such as Kenwood mixers or Tefal and Prestige sets, bought by a trusted UK contact, checked against recall lists, and shipped by per-kg cargo. On heavy, valuable items, freight is a smaller share of the price, which makes the UK route less bad. Remember that many UK-brand appliances are made in China anyway, so the premium is for the brand, not the origin. You can also get some of that "trusted brand" feeling by putting your own brand on Chinese stock. UK supply arrives in irregular, mixed lots, so it can never feed resellers who need the same item every month. China (or authorised distributors of new branded stock) has to stay the core.

## Resellers join when they carry no stock and sell every week

Your past reseller attempts most likely failed for structural reasons. Each unit cost a lot of capital, the item turned over slowly, prices were public, and there was no volume reward. I found no Nigerian study of why reseller schemes fail, so that diagnosis is my reasoning from the prices above. The fix is to take the risk off the reseller. Nigeria already has "sell without stock" infrastructure. Apps like Tendo connect resellers to wholesalers, who handle delivery and payment ([Africa.com, via search summary](https://africa.com/ghanaian-startup-plans-to-takeover-the-region/)), and tools like Bumpa publish dropshipping guides for social-media vendors ([Bumpa](https://www.getbumpa.com/blog/how-to-start-a-successful-dropshipping-business-online-in-nigeria)). Your version is simple. Give resellers a WhatsApp catalogue with photos and a price list that has their margin built in. They collect payment or a deposit, and you deliver directly to their customer in neutral or their branding.

Layer three things on top. The first is **low-ticket, fast items** (Pick #2) as the reseller's everyday product, with mixers and equipment as occasional bigger earners. The second is **clear price tiers** that replace the 3-unit cap: single-unit retail price, a better price at 3+, better again at 10+ or a full carton on consumables. No Nigerian source documented typical tier levels, so set yours from your own margins (see below). The third is **pre-order "shipping windows"**. Announce that the order closes on the 15th and arrives in about 5–7 weeks by sea, take a **30–50% deposit**, which is the commonly recommended range ([Kolonell](https://kolonell.com/en/blog/preorder-backorder-ecommerce-nairobi-2026)), and order only what was paid for plus a small buffer. Resellers then have a deadline to gather orders against, and you stop funding stock that might sit. Be honest about timelines, though. Late delivery is the biggest risk with this model, and many people have lost money treating mini-importation as easy ([Entrepreneurs.ng](https://entrepreneurs.ng/?p=18646)).

Your best resellers probably won't be generic Instagram vendors. They will be people who already gather your buyers. **Baking and catering schools** charge students about **₦300,000** per course ([Jiji QDG](https://jiji.ng/ha/ajah/classes-and-courses/catering-academy-ynzBYd8JCnsZ32OgECO30WYl.html)) and produce a fresh group of new food entrepreneurs every cohort. Give instructors a referral code, a commission, and a demo machine for class. **Empowerment programmes** run by states, NGOs, churches and companies buy starter packs in bulk. They need you registered (CAC, tax) and they move slowly, but one contract can equal months of single sales. **Equipment dealers** at Alaba and Ikeja, such as Kcee Food Equipments, already sell and service bakery and catering equipment ([Finelib](https://www.finelib.com/cities/lagos/business/food/bakery/bakery-equipment)). They are natural partners for a formal brokerage deal, which leads to the broker side of your business.

**Formalise the brokerage.** Replace the ₦20,000 flat fee with a written agreement with 2–3 dealers. The agreement should include a shared price list and a photo catalogue you can rebrand, the dealer delivering and installing, and a percentage commission paid when the dealer receives payment. Something in the **3–5% range** is my illustrative suggestion; I found no sourced Nigerian norm. Then let sub-brokers (instructors, caterers) earn part of that commission. That gives you a reseller channel where nobody holds stock at all.

For delivery, stop advertising blanket free shipping. Lagos last-mile drops cost about **₦1,500–₦5,000** depending on zone ([Kolonell](https://kolonell.com/en/blog/last-mile-delivery-zones-pricing-lagos-2026)), and GIG offers 24–48h interstate delivery to major cities ([GIGL](https://giglogistics.com/gofaster/)). Offer free delivery only within a defined Lagos zone or above an order value (a kit, or a mixer plus consumables), and publish flat rates by zone and state for everything else. Offer park waybill as the cheap option where the buyer pays at pickup. It is affordable but less protected, and losses do happen ([Intercity](https://intercity.ng/blog/how-waybill-works-in-the-nigerian-system)), so say so plainly. Offer an insured courier as the safe option. Build delivery into bundle prices rather than giving it away on single units.

## The plan: two lines, a China core, clear margins, and 90 days of cheap tests

**Product directions.** Build Pick #1, food-vendor starter kits (electric fryer, bain-marie/food warmer, griddle, commercial blender, plus your brokered ovens and tables), and Pick #2, fast-moving consumables and smallware (boards, boxes, food packs, piping bags, nozzles, turntables, your pans, and mixer spares). Keep the mixer as the hero item inside the baker's kit, not as a product sold on its own.

**Sourcing model.** Source consumables locally at first to prove demand, then import the winners. Buy food-vendor equipment and mixers from China through a Lagos-based buying agent or 1688 consolidator in small mixed LCL lots, against pre-order deposits. Register a SONCAP Product Certificate for the electrical models you plan to reorder, so one certificate covers a year of repeat shipments. Keep heavy local items (stainless tables, deck ovens) on the brokerage model. Run UK clearance only as an optional, small, tested test lot of premium branded pieces through someone you trust in the UK.

**Pricing and margin logic.** Work from landed cost, not from competitors' prices. Here is an illustrative China mixer with every figure estimated. FOB $39–65 at ₦1,331/$ is ₦52k–87k. Add freight of ₦7k–21k (assuming 0.05–0.08 CBM per box at $100–195/CBM; the box size is my estimate). Add about 31% of that CIF value in duty and taxes (assuming the 20% band; verify), which is ₦18k–33k. Before agent fees and clearing, that puts landed cost at **roughly ₦77k–141k**, against Nigerian retail prices of ₦145k–180k. The margin could be anywhere from almost nothing to about ₦65k. That is why one free interstate delivery (₦12k–18k) can wipe out a sale. Put your real numbers into this, item by item.

From there, I suggest these working rules. They are my rules of thumb, not sourced benchmarks. Aim for a 40–60% gross margin on consumables and smallware, so a reseller discount of 15–25% still leaves you a healthy profit. Aim for 20–30% on equipment and kits, where the naira profit per sale is larger. Never offer free delivery unless the order's margin is at least three times the delivery cost.

| Price layer | How to set it (illustrative) |
|---|---|
| Your landed cost | Purchase + agent fee + freight + duty/VAT/levies + clearing + SONCAP share + expected defects |
| Retail price | Landed cost ÷ (1 − target margin), then checked against Jiji/Jumia |
| Reseller tier 1 (3+ units) | Retail minus ~10–15% |
| Reseller tier 2 (10+ / carton) | Retail minus ~20–25% (consumables only) |
| No-stock reseller / referral | Commission ~8–12% of sale on equipment; fixed naira amount on kits |
| Broker deals | ~3–5% of dealer price, in writing, paid when the dealer receives payment |
| Delivery | Free within Lagos zone above threshold; flat published rates elsewhere |

**90-day test plan.** The targets in the right column are suggested starting points for judging success. Adjust them to your own numbers.

| Days | What you do | What "working" looks like |
|---|---|---|
| 1–30: fix the foundations | Work out the true landed cost and delivery cost of every current item. End blanket free shipping and publish delivery rules. Ask a licensed customs broker for duty rates on HS 8509, 8516, 8419, 7323 and 7615, and for current SONCAP costs. Sign written commission agreements with 2–3 oven/table dealers. Build a WhatsApp Business catalogue with three tiers. Source 10–15 consumable items locally. Choose 4–6 food-vendor equipment items from a China agent and get samples or detailed specs. | You know your margin on every item; 2 dealer agreements signed; catalogue live |
| 31–60: launch and recruit | Open the first pre-order window for the food-vendor kit and baker's graduate kit, with 30–50% deposits. Recruit 10 no-stock resellers, at least 3 of them baking/catering instructors, each with a referral code. Offer past mixer buyers a "restock" consumables bundle. Post kit demos and customer stories on Instagram/WhatsApp. Optional: a 50–100kg UK test lot of tested, branded pieces, checked against recall lists. | Deposits cover at least 1 CBM of China stock; at least 3 resellers make a sale; first repeat consumables orders |
| 61–90: measure and decide | Receive the first China batch and track delivery times honestly. Calculate gross margin after delivery for each line, reseller activity, consumables reorder rate, and UK-lot sell-through and profit per kg. Keep the top items and drop the rest. Register for empowerment-programme supplier lists if the kits are selling. | Consumables reorder within 30 days; kits profitable after delivery; a clear decision on whether to scale China LCL and whether UK earns a permanent slot |

## Conclusion

The way out of the cake box is to change what you are known for. A one-to-one mixer seller competes only on price against thousands of Jiji listings. A supplier who equips people to start and run food businesses can win on kits, spare parts, honest delivery and a network of instructors and dealers who send buyers to you. Your existing pieces (Chinese suppliers, baker customers, oven and table dealers, local pans) are already most of that business; they just haven't been put together. The big unknowns are exact duty rates by HS code, current SONCAP costs, real deck-oven prices and how fast small food-vendor segments are actually growing. Each of those can be answered cheaply in the first 30 days. Of everything in this report, the deposit-funded shipping window is probably the change that matters most. It turns your slowest problem, cash tied up in stock nobody has ordered yet, into the thing that gives resellers a reason to work with you.
